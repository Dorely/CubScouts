## Lesson Structure

### Opening Circle (5 minutes)
- **Welcome:** "Patriotic Bears ready for citizenship!"
- **Flag Recognition:** Properly display U.S. flag and show respect
- **Citizenship Connection:** "Today we learn about our country and how to serve"
- **Opening:** Pledge of Allegiance with proper flag etiquette

### Activity 1: U.S. Flag Education & Demonstration (18 minutes)
**Requirement 1:** Familiarize yourself with the flag of the United States of America including the history, demonstrating how to raise and lower the flag, how to properly fold and display, and the United States flag etiquette

**Flag History (6 minutes):**
- Original 13 colonies and first flag design
- Evolution to current 50-star flag
- Significance of colors: red (hardiness/valor), white (purity/innocence), blue (vigilance/perseverance/justice)
- Flag Day (June 14) and other flag-related observances

**Flag Handling Demonstration (8 minutes):**

**Proper Raising and Lowering:**
- Raise flag quickly and with purpose
- Lower flag slowly and ceremoniously
- Never let flag touch the ground
- Raise to full staff, then lower to half-staff when appropriate

**Flag Folding Practice:**
- Demonstrate proper triangle folding technique
- Each scout participates in folding demonstration
- Explain significance of 13 folds tradition
- Practice with proper respect and care

**Flag Display Rules:**
- Display from sunrise to sunset (or properly illuminated at night)
- In bad weather, only all-weather flags should be displayed
- Flag should be displayed on federal holidays
- Proper positioning with other flags

**Flag Etiquette (4 minutes):**
- Stand and remove hats during Pledge of Allegiance
- Place right hand over heart during pledge
- Proper respect during flag ceremonies
- What to do when flag becomes worn or damaged

### Activity 2: U.S. Symbols Identification & Art Creation (20 minutes)
**Requirements 2 & 3:** Identify 3 symbols that represent the United States. Pick your favorite and make a model, work of art, or other craft that depicts the symbol

**U.S. Symbols Exploration (8 minutes):**

**Common U.S. Symbols:**
- Bald Eagle (national bird, strength, freedom)
- Statue of Liberty (freedom, welcome to immigrants)
- Liberty Bell (independence, freedom)
- Capitol Building (democracy, government)
- Mount Rushmore (leadership, history)
- Uncle Sam (American spirit, patriotism)
- American Flag (unity, values, identity)

**Symbol Selection:**
- Each scout chooses 3 symbols to identify
- Discuss what each symbol represents
- Learn brief history of chosen symbols
- Select favorite symbol for art project

**Art Creation Project (12 minutes):**

**Art Options:**
- Drawing or painting chosen symbol
- Clay or playdough sculpture
- Collage using magazines/materials
- 3D model using craft supplies
- Digital art or poster design

**Project Requirements:**
- Include symbol's name and what it represents
- Add personal reflection on why symbol is meaningful
- Demonstrate understanding of symbol's significance
- Create something to share with family

### Activity 3: Nonprofit Organization Learning (12 minutes)
**Requirement 4:** Learn about the mission of any non-profit. Find out how they fund their activities and how volunteers are used to help

**Local Nonprofit Exploration:**
- Present information about 2-3 local nonprofits
- Examples: food banks, animal shelters, environmental groups, youth organizations
- Discuss missions and how they help community

**Nonprofit Education:**

**What is a Nonprofit?**
- Organizations that help community without making profit
- Money goes back into helping more people
- Serve important community needs
- Rely on donations and volunteers

**Funding Sources:**
- Individual donations from community members
- Grants from foundations and government
- Fundraising events and campaigns
- Corporate sponsorships
- Membership fees (some organizations)

**Volunteer Roles:**
- Direct service (serving food, cleaning, helping animals)
- Administrative support (organizing, data entry)
- Special events (fundraisers, awareness campaigns)
- Skills-based volunteering (professional expertise)
- Youth volunteers (age-appropriate activities)

**Connection to Scouting:**
- How Scouting serves as community organization
- Ways Scouts can volunteer and help nonprofits
- Importance of giving back to community

### Activity 5: Service Project Planning (5 minutes)
**Requirement 5:** Participate in a service project

**Service Project Selection:**
- Choose community service project for next week
- Connect to nonprofit organization learned about today
- Plan how den can make meaningful contribution
- Ensure project is age-appropriate and achievable

**Project Ideas:**
- Food drive for local food bank
- Supplies collection for animal shelter
- Environmental cleanup in community
- Cards/crafts for nursing home residents
- School supplies for children in need

**Planning Details:**
- Set date and time for service project
- Determine what supplies are needed
- Plan transportation and supervision
- Prepare to document service for next meeting

## Notes for Den Leader

### Flag Education Tips:
- Use actual flag for all demonstrations
- Emphasize respect and proper handling
- Connect to previous Scout skills (ceremonies, respect)
- Consider inviting veteran or active military member

### Safety Considerations:
- Supervise all flag handling activities
- Ensure proper respect during flag activities
- Monitor art projects for safe tool use
- Maintain appropriate reverence during patriotic activities

### Civic Education Approach:
- Keep political content neutral and age-appropriate
- Focus on community service and civic responsibility
- Emphasize how citizens can make positive difference
- Connect to Scout Law values
