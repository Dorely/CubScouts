## Lesson Structure

### Opening Circle (5 minutes)
- **Welcome Back:** "Our camping bears are back!"
- **Adventure Recap:** Quick sharing of favorite camping moments
- **Nature Connection:** "We learned to live like bears in their habitat"
- **Opening:** Scout Oath emphasizing our duty to be "helpful" to nature

### Activity 1: Camping Adventure Show & Tell (15 minutes)
**Purpose:** Review outdoor learning and celebrate discoveries

**Photo Gallery Walk:**
- Display photos from Adventure Weekend around room
- Scouts identify themselves and share memories
- Point out examples of Leave No Trace principles practiced during the walk
- Celebrate successful completion of outdoor requirements

**Nature Journal Sharing:**
- Each scout shares one favorite discovery from their journal
- Show sketches and photos taken during camping
- Discuss six signs of wildlife identified during the walk (mammals, birds, insects, reptiles)
- Highlight examples of Leave No Trace principles practiced

**Adventure Achievement Recognition:**
- Review all Bear Habitat requirements completed during camping
- Acknowledge growth in outdoor skills and confidence
- Present Bear Habitat completion certificates
- Celebrate environmental stewardship

### Activity 2: Food Chain Discovery Activity (25 minutes)
**Purpose:** Understand ecosystem connections observed during camping

**Introduction to Food Chains (5 minutes):**
- "Remember the animals and plants we saw camping?"
- "Everything in nature is connected - let's discover how!"
- Bears are omnivores - they eat both plants and animals
- We observed this ecosystem connection during our adventure

**Local Ecosystem Food Chain Building (15 minutes):**

**Primary Producers (Plants):**
- Oak trees, grass, wildflowers seen during camping
- These make their own food from sunlight
- Foundation of all food chains

**Primary Consumers (Herbivores):**
- Rabbits, squirrels, deer observed or signs found
- These eat plants for energy
- Connect to plant discoveries from weekend

**Secondary Consumers (Carnivores/Omnivores):**
- Hawks, owls, coyotes, bears (signs or evidence found)
- These eat other animals
- Include insects like spiders that eat other insects

**Interactive Food Chain Game (5 minutes):**
- Scouts become different parts of food chain
- Act out who eats what
- Show how energy flows through ecosystem
- Demonstrate what happens if one part is removed

### Activity 3: Ecosystem Impact & Responsibility (10 minutes)
**Purpose:** Connect Leave No Trace principles to ecosystem health

**Discussion Questions:**
- "How did our Leave No Trace practices protect these food chains?"
- "What would happen if we left trash in animal habitats?"
- "Why is it important to leave natural objects where we find them?"

**Environmental Stewardship Pledge:**
- Create group commitment to protecting habitats
- Each scout shares one way they'll help protect nature
- Connect to upcoming outdoor adventures
- Emphasize role as environmental protectors

**Real-World Connections:**
- Discuss local environmental issues age-appropriately
- Talk about how Scouts can make a positive difference
- Connect to upcoming service projects

### Activity 4: Preparation for Future Adventures (5 minutes)
**Purpose:** Build on Bear Habitat experience for upcoming activities

**Joshua Tree Campout Preview (November 7-9):**

**Skill Building Goals:**

**Connection to Other Adventures:**
