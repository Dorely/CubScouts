# Modular Design Lesson Plan

**Rank:** Webelos (Elective) | **Estimated Time:** 2 den meetings × 60 min (+ auxiliary if needed) | **Group:** 7–9 scouts, classroom + outdoor flex

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ADVANCE PREP — READ FIRST**

Print video discussion sheets and parent-permission verification list. Test playback and site filters before Scouts arrive.

No consumables — bricks are reused Day 2.

## Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Req 1:** Learn what modular design is and identify three things that use modular design in their construction.
- [ ] **Req 2:** Using modular-based building pieces, build a model without a set of instructions.
- [ ] **Req 3:** Using the model made in requirement 2, create a set of step-by-step instructions on how to make your model.
- [ ] **Req 4:** Have someone make your model using your instructions.
- [ ] **Req 5:** Using the same modular pieces used in requirement 2, build another model of something different.
- [ ] **Req 6:** With your parent or legal guardian's permission, watch a video demonstrating how something was built using modular design.

## Day 1 — What Is Modular? & Free Build (60 min) — low-coordination Reqs only

*Classroom with tables. Groups of 3. No video.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Flag, Scout Oath & Law (focus on *thrifty* — conserve resources through modular reuse). Preview Modular Design as the building elective. Explain Day 1 = learn what modular means + build a model without instructions; Day 2 = write instructions, have someone test them, build a second different model, and watch a modular-build video with parent permission. Show poster with LEGO as the anchor example.

**Activity 1 — Modular Concept & Three Examples (15 min) — Req 1**
- *What Makes It Modular? (7 min):* Leader presents definition — modular design = building with standardized interchangeable pieces/units that combine in many ways, conserve resources, and allow repair/reuse. Contrast with single-pour or custom-carved. Show LEGO → furniture (shelving units) → building (shipping-container homes) as the three starter examples, with photos.
- *Scout Identify (8 min):* In groups of 3, Scouts brainstorm and name three things that use modular design — den must reach consensus on three to record (e.g., LEGO/brick sets, sectional sofa, container building, computer with swappable parts, playground panels). Each group shares one; leader posts den list with quick why it's modular (standard size, repeatable, reconfigurable). Each Scout copies the three to worksheet. Completes Req 1 — each Scout has learned the concept and identified three modular examples.

**Activity 2 — Build Without Instructions (35 min) — Req 2**
- *Challenge Brief (5 min):* Distribute trays — same piece count per group, no instruction booklet. Challenge: build a model of anything you imagine (vehicle, house, creature, bridge) using only the pieces in your tray. Rules: work together, 20 min build, model must use at least 15 pieces and be freestanding, keep pieces on mats.
- *Build Time (20 min):* Groups of 3 collaborate — one suggests, one finds pieces, one assembles, rotating roles. Adults circulate but do not design; they prompt ("what's the base? how will that stay?"). Each Scout must place pieces personally — leader verifies hands-on participation.
- *Gallery & Name (10 min):* Teams display models on mats, name them, and each Scout explains one design choice (e.g., "we used plates for the roof so it lifts off"). Photograph each model for Req 3 reference (leader or Scout with permission). Leave models intact on trays, covered with paper — they are the source for Day 2 instructions. Completes Req 2 — each Scout built a model without instructions as part of a team (individual build also accepted if pieces allow — keep group of 3 for instruction writing regardless).

**Closing (5 min)**
Recap modular idea (reuse, repair, reconfigure) and today's builds. Preview Day 2: each group will write step-by-step instructions for the model they just built, swap and test, build a second different model from the same pieces, and watch a video — bring pencils, expect to draw/write clearly. Confirm parent permission slips for Day 2 video and that models stay untouched. Collect attendance and photos.

## Day 2 — Write, Test, Rebuild & Watch (60 min) — Advanced / Coordinated Reqs HERE

*Classroom with tables. Devices + projector for video. Groups of 3 with same trays/models.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Reveal covered models, re-state Digital Safety ground rules for video portion (stay on approved site, no searching alone, adult controls playback). Verify parent/guardian permission list for Req 6 — any Scout without permission will watch with leader summary alternative and get take-home video option. Assign roles for instruction writing.

**Activity 1 — Write Step-by-Step Instructions (20 min) — Req 3**
- *How to Write Good Instructions (5 min):* Leader shows a 4-step example (numbered, one action per step, simple words + drawing/photo). Emphasize order, piece counts, and orientation cues ("add 2x4 red plate on top of blue base, studs aligned").
- *Create Instructions (15 min):* Groups of 3 disassemble model conceptually (or refer to photos) and rebuild on paper — each group produces one instruction set with at least 4 numbered steps, each step with a drawing or photo-box + written action. Each Scout authors at least one step in own handwriting/drawing. Keep instructions clear enough for another Scout to follow without seeing the model. Adults coach clarity, not content. Models remain assembled as reference until instructions are done, then gently disassemble into tray for testing. Completes Req 3 — each group has created step-by-step instructions from their Req 2 model.

**Activity 2 — Test the Instructions & Build Something Different (20 min) — Req 4 & Req 5**
- *Swap and Build — Req 4 (12 min):* Groups swap instruction sheets (A→B, B→C, C→A) and attempt to rebuild the other group's model using only the instructions — no peeking at photos beyond what's on the sheet. One rebuilder, one reader, one checker per group. After 8 min, compare rebuild to photo/original — note what was clear and one improvement. Each group's rebuilder demonstrates following instructions successfully for at least 3 steps. Completes Req 4 — someone made the model using written instructions.
- *Second Build — Same Pieces, Different Model — Req 5 (8 min):* Using the same pieces just tested, each group now builds another model of something different from Req 2 (e.g., if first was a house, now a boat). Require at least 15 pieces and a different purpose/shape. Each Scout places pieces and team names the new model. Leader verifies different from first via photos. Completes Req 5 — second distinct model from same modular pieces.

**Activity 3 — Watch a Modular-Build Video (10 min) — Req 6**
- *Viewing (6 min):* With parent/guardian permission verified, den watches 1–2 previewed videos (e.g., time-lapse of modular home set, factory building container housing, or designer assembling modular furniture/shelter) on the big screen — adult controls search and playback, no Scout browsing. Leader pauses once to name modular units visible.
- *Discussion (4 min):* In groups of 3, Scouts name one thing the video built modularly and how standard units sped the build or conserved resources. Each Scout shares one observation; leader initials Req 6. For Scouts without permission, provide take-home video link with parent to watch together and return with signed note — do not require solo viewing. Completes Req 6 — watched with parent/guardian permission.

**Closing (5 min)**
Gallery of second builds and instruction sheets side-by-side — circle what made instructions work (numbers, clear drawings, piece counts). Recap modular benefits (conserve resources, repair one unit not whole thing). Thank adults, collect instruction sheets + photos for records, and bag/sort bricks for next den. Remind families to return video permission notes if completed at home.

## Auxiliary Day (if needed)

**No auxiliary required.** All requirements finish in the two den meetings. If a Scout misses Day 1, have them build a quick Req 2 model in the first 15 min of Day 2 with a spare tray, photograph it, then join their group for instruction writing (group of 4 is fine). If a Scout misses Day 2, schedule a 30-min makeup with their Day 1 model photo and a spare tray to write/test/second-build and watch the video with parent present — do not sign off Req 3–6 without live instruction creation and peer testing.

## Materials Summary

" What is modular" poster + 3 real-world example photos, LEGO-type brick sets/bins (3 trays of 80–120 pieces, bulk bin split), building mats/trays (3), build challenge cards, instruction-template sheets (12), peer-test checklists (12), pencils/markers/rulers, zip sorting bags, device + projector/speaker + internet + curated modular-build videos (bookmarked), Digital Safety one-pager, parent-permission slips/verification list, attendance sheet. Optional: camera/phone for model photos, printed step-example poster.

## Safety Notes

Use Scouting America SAFE Checklist at every meeting. Review Guide to Safe Scouting and Age-Appropriate Guidelines. Before any video, review Digital Safety and Online Scouting Activities — adults control search/playback, preview all videos for age-appropriateness, use filtered/approved sites, and verify parent/guardian permission for every Scout before showing (take-home alternative for those without). Keep small pieces away from mouths, enforce sharing and turn-taking in groups of 3, and store bricks sorted to avoid trip hazards. Follow manufacturer's literature and the most restrictive local rule. Have first-aid kit on hand for minor pinch/scrape; ensure adequate adult coverage and no solo online browsing.
