# Aware and Care Lesson Plan

**Rank:** Webelos (Elective) | **Estimated Time:** 2 den meetings × 60 min (+ auxiliary make-up if needed) | **Group:** 7–9 scouts, classroom + outdoor flex

## Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Req 1:** Do an activity that shows the challenges of a being visually impaired.
- [ ] **Req 2:** Do an activity that shows the challenges of being hearing impaired.
- [ ] **Req 3:** Explore barriers to access.

## Day 1 — Seeing, Hearing & Access Barriers (60 min) — Reqs 1, 2, 3 — low-coordination only

*Classroom + hallway/outdoor flex for obstacle and scavenger. Groups of 3. No guest.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Flag, Scout Oath & Law (focus on *kind* and *helpful*), preview Aware and Care — learning by experiencing challenges respectfully. Set ground rules: respectful language, no mimicking as jokes, every person's experience is different, we listen to learn how to help well.

**Activity 1 — Visual Impairment Stations (20 min) — Req 1**
Rotate groups of 3 through 3 stations × 6 min:
- *Can You Draw It? + What Is This? (6 min):* With both eyes covered (blindfold) or one eye covered then blurred, draw a house/tree or identify bagged objects by touch only; then discuss what was hard and what helped (verbal cues, patience, asking permission before guiding).
- *I Can Do It (6 min):* Pour a cup of water and eat a small snack without using vision — partner guides verbally without touching unless asked. Debrief independence and offering help the right way.
- *Can We Get There? (8 min):* Short indoor obstacle (cones/chairs/string) navigated with blindfold and a sighted peer giving directions. Switch roles. Leader ties each to Req 1: challenges of being visually impaired and helpful behaviors. Completes Req 1.

**Activity 2 — Hearing Impairment Stations (15 min) — Req 2**
- *Listen Carefully / Simon Says (7 min):* Play Simon Says wearing noise-canceling headphones/earplugs — Scouts notice missed cues and need for visual signals. Repeat with face-toward-speaker vs. face-away to feel lip-reading difficulty.
- *What Animal + ASL (8 min):* Guess the animal without talking (charades/mime only) and learn 5–6 ASL signs from alphabet/story chart; one Scout tells a one-sentence story in signs while others decode. Discuss challenges of being hearing impaired and respectful communication. Completes Req 2.

**Activity 3 — Barriers to Access Hunt (15 min) — Req 3**
- *Can You Reach It? (5 min):* Place object on high shelf, behind door, or across taped "curb" — Scouts in chairs or without using hands try to reach it; note barriers.
- *Outdoors Barriers to Access (10 min):* Teams of 3 with clipboard walk classroom + hallway/outside doorway listing barriers (stairs without ramp, heavy door, high counter, narrow path, no signage, cluttered floor). Report one barrier and one fix (ramp, lower handle, clear path, visual + audio cues). Completes Req 3.

**Closing (5 min)**
Circle reflection: What surprised you about each challenge? What's one way to be helpful without assuming? Preview Day 2 guest — how to welcome, listen, and ask respectful questions. Thank adults and tidy stations.

## Day 2 — Guest Visit: Listening, Learning & Respect (60 min) — Advanced / coordinated requirements HERE

*Classroom in circle. Guest present. Senior leader hosts. Service-dog friendly layout if applicable.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Welcome, introductions by first name, den explains Aware and Care and what they did Day 1. Review Q&A etiquette: ask person-first, listen fully, one question at a time, thank for sharing.

**Activity 1 — Guest Conversation (30 min) — Req 4**
Host-led with guest (person with disability, rehab/special-ed professional, or service-dog trainer/handler):
- *Obstacles & Strategies (15 min):* Guest shares daily obstacles and how they navigate them — mobility, vision/hearing, communication, attitudes — and tools/supports that help (cane, wheelchair, hearing tech, service dog tasks). For service-dog option, handler demonstrates 2–3 tasks and explains jobs for dogs, etiquette (ask before petting, do not distract).
- *Q&A (15 min):* Scouts in groups of 3 prepared one thoughtful question each; host screens for respect, ensures everyone who wants to asks. Focus on "how can we be helpful and inclusive at school and Scouts?" Leader records key takeaways on poster. Completes Req 4.

**Activity 2 — Reflect & Act (20 min) — Req 4 continued**
- *Thank & Reflect (10 min):* Scouts write/draw thank-you cards (one per Scout, plus group card) noting one thing learned and one action they will take (e.g., clear a blocked doorway, face a person when speaking, offer help by asking first, include everyone in games).
- *Small-Group Action Plans (10 min):* Teams of 3 pick one barrier from Day 1 and propose a fix they can do this month at home, school, or den (rearrange, label, add visual cues, buddy up). Share one plan per group aloud; leader collects for den record.

**Closing (5 min)**
Thank guest (and service dog handler if present) with cards and den cheer. Recap: challenges are real, people adapt with skill, our job is kindness and access. Explain auxiliary make-up only needed if a Scout missed the guest — they can attend the backup visit or small-group make-up interview.

## Auxiliary Day (if needed)

**Optional Auxiliary Visit (45–60 min) — Only if Day 2 guest was cancelled or a Scout missed Day 2.**

If the scheduled guest cancels or a Scout is absent, run a 3rd auxiliary session outside regular den time: visit a rehabilitation facility, invite the backup guest to a weekend slot, or host a service-dog training site demo. Keep the same safety and sensitivity plan — confirm accessibility, animal policy, AHMRs if off-site, and 2-deep leadership plus chaperones. Scouts interview the host using the same talking points and write thank-you notes. No additional requirements beyond Req 4; this day simply ensures every Scout can meet someone and hear first-hand about obstacles and strategies.

## Safety Notes

Use Scouting America SAFE Checklist at every meeting. Review Guide to Safe Scouting and Age-Appropriate Guidelines before any obstacle or off-site visit; secure 2-deep leadership and parent permissions for any auxiliary site. Before starting, review Digital Safety and Online Scouting Activities if Scouts research locally. For impairment simulations, keep activities brief, voluntary, and in safe areas — no running blindfolded, spotters at all times, clear floor, and stop if a Scout is uncomfortable. Emphasize respectful, person-first language; never frame simulations as "knowing what it's like" — frame as building empathy and awareness. If a service dog visits, confirm allergies, ask before petting, no feeding, and keep dog on leash with handler control. Be aware of local regulations that supersede Scouting America practices.
