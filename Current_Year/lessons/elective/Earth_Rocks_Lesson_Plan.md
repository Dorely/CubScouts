## Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Req 1:** Examine the three types of rocks, sedimentary, igneous, and metamorphic.
- [ ] **Req 2:** Find a rock, safely break it apart and examine it.
- [ ] **Req 3:** Make a mineral test kit and test minerals according to the Mohs scale of mineral hardness. Using the rock cycle chart or one like it, discuss how hardness determines which materials can be used in homes, in landscapes, or for recreation.
- [ ] **Req 4:** Grow a crystal.

## Day 1 — Rock Types & Mohs Hardness (60 min) — low-coordination Reqs only

*Classroom with tables. No hammers/cracking. Groups of 3.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Flag, Scout Oath & Law (focus on *curiosity* and *clean* — observe closely, leave workspace clean). Preview Earth Rocks as the geology elective. Explain Day 1 = examine rock types and test hardness at tables; Day 2 = safely break geode/rock + start crystal (eye protection + hammers outdoors, crystal finishes at home). Show rock-cycle poster.

**Activity 1 — Examine the Three Rock Types (25 min) — Req 1**
- *Crayon Rock Formation Demo (10 min):* With crayon shavings and warm hands/foil wrap, demo sedimentary (layer and press), igneous (melt and cool), metamorphic (heat + pressure and flatten). Connect to real process on rock-cycle chart — melting, cooling, weathering, compaction, heat/pressure.
- *Classification Hands-On (15 min):* Groups of 3 rotate labeled specimens — sedimentary (sandstone — grainy/layered, limestone — light/fizzes with vinegar demo by leader), igneous (granite — speckled crystals, obsidian — glassy, pumice — light/bubbly), metamorphic (slate — flat sheets, marble — crystalline). Each Scout handles, names type, and records one clue (texture, crystal size, layers). Invite brief "rock expert" talk if available, otherwise leader narrates formation story. Teams sort mixed pile into three trays and justify choices. Completes Req 1 — each Scout has examined and named sedimentary, igneous, and metamorphic.

**Activity 2 — Mohs Mineral Test Lab (25 min) — Req 3**
- *Build the Kit (5 min):* Each group of 3 assembles a test kit — streak plate, penny (hardness ~3.5), steel nail (~5.5), glass plate (~5.5–6), plus fingernail (~2.5) and printed Mohs scale (talc 1 → diamond 10). Show rock-cycle chart for hardness conversation anchor.
- *Test Rotation (15 min):* At 3 stations, Scouts test 4–5 minerals (e.g., talc, gypsum, calcite, quartz, feldspar) — attempt scratch with each tool, check streak on plate, record "scratches / does not scratch." Leader demos correct technique (firm one-direction scratch, check for groove vs. powder). Each Scout tests at least two minerals personally. Emphasize eye protection not needed here but hands washed after.
- *Hardness Discussion (5 min):* Using rock-cycle chart, discuss how hardness drives use — soft gypsum/talc = drywall/chalk (easy to shape), hard granite/quartz = countertops/building (resists scratch), mid hardness = landscape stone. Groups name one home, one landscape, one recreation example (e.g., marble countertop, slate patio, granite climbing holds). Completes Req 3 — each Scout made/used a kit, tested by Mohs, and discussed hardness-based uses.

**Closing (5 min)**
Recap three rock types and Mohs idea (harder scratches softer). Preview Day 2: bring safety glasses if issued, breaking geodes outdoors with hammer/chisel behind tape line, then mixing crystals that grow at home over days. Confirm geodes arrived and parent permission for hot-water step handled by adults only. Collect observation sheets.

## Day 2 — Break a Geode & Grow a Crystal (60 min) — Advanced / Coordinated Reqs HERE

*First half outdoors or garage on hard surface (eye protection + hammers); second half indoors at tables (crystal). Adults 1:3 for hammer work.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Safety briefing: eye protection on entire break session, one striker at a time, others behind tape line 6 ft back, gloves on hammer hand, check that no one is in swing path, adults handle chisel placement. Show crystal timeline poster — mix today, observe over 3–7 days, photos next week.

**Activity 1 — Break Apart and Examine (25 min) — Req 2**
- *Setup (5 min):* Adults place geode in heavy sock, wrap with duct tape, set on drop cloth over concrete. Mark strike circle and spectator line. Review "find a rock" — these geodes were sourced for the den to safely crack.
- *Break Rotation (15 min):* Groups of 3 rotate to breaking station — one Scout per turn wears eye protection + gloves, holds perimeter while adult positions chisel, adult or Scout under direct hand-over-hand guidance taps with hammer until crack, then open. Others observe from behind line with eye protection. Each group cracks 1 geode; leader assists every strike. Never let Scouts swing freely without chisel control.
- *Examine (5 min):* At tables (eye protection still on until fragments bagged), Scouts examine fresh faces with magnifiers — crystal color, hollow vs. solid, banding. Each Scout records one observation and whether it looks sedimentary/igneous-leaning or crystal-filled. Bag fragments immediately, wipe tables, remove gloves. Completes Req 2 — each Scout found (was provided), safely broke apart (supervised with protection), and examined a rock/geode.

**Activity 2 — Grow a Crystal (25 min) — Req 4**
- *Mix Demo (10 min):* Adults only handle hot water — leader heats and pours (kettle at adult station), Scouts measure alum/borax/sugar per printed recipe, stir until dissolved, add food coloring optionally, suspend string from pencil across jar mouth. Each group of 3 mixes one jar; Scouts do measuring/stirring/suspending, adults do pouring. Review that food and drinks stay away from experiment.
- *Observation Plan (10 min):* Scouts label jars with name/date, record starting state on observation log (color, clarity, string position), predict growth. Explain home care: keep jar still, warm, uncovered, observe daily, do not drink. Sweet-crystal (sugar/rock candy) variant if food-safe desired — note longer timeline and hygiene.
- *Home Finish Note (5 min):* Hand out crystal instructions + photo share sheet. Crystal will finish at home over days — bring photo or jar to next den meeting for check-in. Completes Req 4 start; sign-off after Scout shows growth next week (or live if rapid alum/borax shows overnight nucleation). Keep instructions for families without internet.

**Closing (5 min)**
Goggles collected and counted, drop cloths folded with fragments contained. Circle: what did the inside of the geode look like, which mineral test reminded you of it, and how does hardness relate to what we broke? Remind Scouts to watch crystals grow at home and not to touch/mouth them. Thank adults and note photo check next week.

## Auxiliary Day (if needed)

**No required auxiliary.** Crystal growth is the only multi-day element — Scouts complete it at home over 3–7 days and share results at the next den meeting or via posted photo. If Day 2 outdoor cracking is weather-blocked, schedule a 45-min auxiliary outdoor crack session within the week with the same eye protection, hammer/chisel, drop cloth, tape line, and 1:3 supervision; do not crack indoors. No pack hike or campout needed for this adventure.

## Materials Summary

Rock-cycle posters/handouts, three-type specimen sets (sedimentary/igneous/metamorphic, 9+ pieces), magnifiers, mineral test kits per group (streak plates, penny, steel nail, glass plate, Mohs cards), mineral samples (talc, gypsum, calcite, quartz, feldspar), crayons + foil for demo, paper towels; Day 2: geodes in socks + duct tape (4–5), hammers (2–3), cold chisels (2), safety glasses (12), work gloves (6), drop cloths, floor tape/markers, trash bags; crystal supplies — alum/borax/sugar, clear jars/cups (12), string, pencils/popsicle sticks, measuring spoons, kettle/hot water (adult only), food coloring, observation logs + instruction sheets, attendance sheet, first-aid kit.

## Safety Notes

Use Scouting America SAFE Checklist at every meeting. Review Guide to Safe Scouting and Age-Appropriate Guidelines. Conduct mineral test and crystal experiments only after leader has rehearsed them; keep food/drinks away from mineral/crystal materials and wash hands after handling. For breaking: wear protective clothing and eye protection for everyone in the area, enforce one striker at a time behind a 6-ft tape line, gloves on hammer hand, control chisel placement by adult, hammer on stable concrete with drop cloth, and collect/shut away fragments immediately. Hot water pouring is adult-only — keep Scouts back, use insulated kettle, and check AHMRs for allergies/sensitivities. Have first-aid kit and incident plan on hand; follow manufacturer's literature and the most restrictive local rule.
