# My Community Lesson Plan

**Rank:** Webelos (Required) | **Estimated Time:** 2 den meetings × 60 min (+ auxiliary service day optional) | **Group:** 7–9 scouts, classroom + outdoor flex

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ADVANCE PREP — READ FIRST**

For Req 4, confirm service project partner: food pantry (*Happy Birthday Bags* or *Scouting for Food*), diaper/formula drive recipient, or *10,000 for the Troops* card drive — get drop-off logistics, collection bins, and complete *Service Project Planning Checklist* + *SAFE Project Tool Use* review. > - **Auxiliary / Pack activity?** Req 4 service project may fit in Day 2 if it is small (card making, bag assembly). If it is a drive or food pantry event, schedule as an auxiliary pack service project (3rd day, 60–90 min) and use Day 2 for guest + prep. Either path counts — note choice in den record.

## Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Req 1:** Learn about majority and plurality types of voting.
- [ ] **Req 2:** Speak with someone who is elected to their position. Discover the type of voting that was used to elect them and why.
- [ ] **Req 3:** Choose a federal law and create a timeline of the history of the law. Include the involvement of the 3 branches of government.
- [ ] **Req 4:** Participate in a service project.

## Day 1 — How We Vote + How Laws Are Made (60 min) — low-coordination requirements only

*Classroom. No guest, no service logistics — table work and voting games.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Flag, Scout Oath & Law (focus on *duty to country*), SAFE Checklist. Explain citizenship: we vote, we learn how laws happen, we serve.

**Activity 1 — Majority vs. Plurality Voting (25 min) — Req 1**
- Mini-lesson (5 min): Define *plurality* (most votes wins, not necessarily >50%) vs. *majority* (>50% wins; may need runoff). Show simple 9-voter examples on board.
- *Snack-lections* (10 min): Present 3 snack choices for a future meeting. Round 1: plurality vote — count, announce plurality winner. Round 2: majority check — did winner have 5 of 9? If not, hold runoff between top two and revote to achieve majority. Discuss why different elections use different rules.
- *Denner Election* demo (10 min): Run a denner vote both ways — first plurality, then majority runoff if needed. Record tally on poster to keep. Debrief: when is plurality efficient vs. when is majority fairer? Check off Req 1 when both types have been practiced and explained.

**Activity 2 — Federal Law Timeline: ADA (25 min) — Req 3**
- Introduce *History of the Americans with Disabilities Act* as the example federal law (10 min): Leader walks timeline cards across wall — legislative path (Congress hearings and bills), executive involvement (signing), judicial interpretation (later court decisions). Hand out branch roles sheet.
- Small groups of 3 build timeline (15 min): Each group places 6–8 events in order (e.g., early disability rights advocacy, congressional introduction, committee votes, passage, presidential signing, implementation, later amendments/court cases). Include which branch acted at each step. Groups present one event each. Completes Req 3 when timeline shows history plus all three branches labeled.

**Closing (5 min)**
Recap voting types and ADA timeline, preview Day 2: elected official visit and service project. Assign: write one question for the elected official about how they were elected and their role.

## Day 2 — Meet Your Government + Serve Your Community (60 min) — Advanced / coordinated requirements HERE

*Classroom with guest seating. Service project may extend to auxiliary if large.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Welcome, introductions, thank guest for coming. Review respectful listening and how to ask questions.

**Activity 1 — Our Elected Official (25 min) — Req 2**
- Guest talk (15 min): Elected official shares office, how they were elected, what type of voting was used (plurality for many local races, majority/runoff in some), and why that system is used locally.
- Q&A (10 min): Scouts ask prepared questions; leader ensures the voting-type question is answered explicitly. Scouts record answer on worksheet. Thank guest with card and photo if permitted. Completes Req 2 when Scouts can state the official's position, voting type used, and reason.

*Backup if guest cancels same day: Visit a public government meeting recording or reschedule guest within 2 weeks — do not substitute with video unless no in-person option exists.*

**Activity 2 — Service Project (25 min) — Req 4**
Choose one that fits your partner and time — small projects finish in den, large drives become auxiliary:

- *In-den options (fits 60 min):* **Happy Birthday Bag Project** (assemble birthday bags with cake mix, frosting, candles, card for food pantry), **10,000 for the Troops** (make holiday cards with messages), or **Diaper and Formula Drive** prep (sort, count, pack donations already collected).
- *If drive/collection:* Use Day 2 to sort, count, and pack items families brought, write thank-you notes to donors, and prepare delivery. Delivery itself may be auxiliary.
- Execute with *Service Project Planning Checklist* and *SAFE Project Tool Use* in effect: train on any tools, continuous adult supervision, age-appropriate tasks, no power tools. Log participation per Scout.

**Closing (5 min)**
Reflection: one thing learned from elected official, one way service helped community. Announce delivery/drop-off plan if service extends to auxiliary day. Collect timeline and voting worksheets for records.

## Auxiliary Day — Service Project Extension (only if needed)

If your chosen service project is a collection drive, pantry drop-off, or larger community event:

- **When:** Within 2 weeks of Day 2, 60–90 min, pack or den.
- **Logistics:** Partner site permission, transportation, adult ratio, weather plan, collection bins, delivery receipt.
- **Program:** Final collection, delivery to partner org, optional tour, group photo, and brief reflection: who was helped and what Scouts would do differently. This auxiliary day completes Req 4 for Scouts who did prep on Day 2 but not delivery.

If you completed a small in-den project (cards, birthday bags) on Day 2, no auxiliary day is needed — mark Req 4 complete in den.

## Materials Summary

## Safety Notes

Use SAFE Checklist every meeting; review Guide to Safe Scouting. For service projects, complete Service Project Planning Checklist before starting, review SAFE Project Tool Use for age-appropriate tool/activity selection, provide tool training and continuous qualified adult supervision, and follow manufacturer's restrictions (most restrictive applies). For guest visits, maintain two-deep leadership, verify guest identity, and keep discussion age-appropriate. Watch Service Projects in Cub Scouting video (6:44) for leader prep. Follow local regulations for food handling and donation drop-offs.
