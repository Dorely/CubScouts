# Champions for Nature Webelos Lesson Plan

**Rank:** Webelos (Elective) | **Estimated Time:** 2 den meetings × 60 min (+ auxiliary if needed) | **Group:** 7–9 scouts, classroom + outdoor flex

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ADVANCE PREP — READ FIRST**

Confirm project type and fit for 7–9 Webelos in 45–60 min: bee hotel / bat house build, waterway cleanup, invasive pull, native planting, or nesting habitat install. Complete Service Project Planning Checklist, pull weather forecast, confirm tools/materials, and recruit 1 adult per 3 Scouts plus site host. Verify AHMRs, tool age-appropriateness, gloves, sunscreen, water, and trash-bag logistics. Have backup date and indoor alternative if weather turns.

State to families: Day 2 is service day; auxiliary is overflow/make-up and for crews who need a larger site.

## Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Req 1:** Discover the four components that make up a habitat: food, water, shelter, space.
- [ ] **Req 2:** Pick an animal that is currently threatened or endangered to complete requirements 3, 4, and 5.
- [ ] **Req 3:** Identify the characteristics that classify an animal as a threatened or endangered species.
- [ ] **Req 4:** Explore what caused this animal to be threatened or endangered.
- [ ] **Req 5:** Research what is currently being done to protect the animal.
- [ ] **Req 6:** Participate in a conservation service project.

---

## Day 1 — Habitat & Species Research Studio (60 min) — Reqs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — low-coordination only

*Classroom + outdoor flex for habitat hunt. Groups of 3. No service tools.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Flag, Scout Oath & Law (focus on *reverent* and *clean* — care for wildlife and habitat), preview Champions for Nature as the habitat + endangered species + service elective. Explain Day 1 = learn habitats and research a threatened/endangered animal, Day 2 = act with a conservation service project. Assign research teams of 3.

**Activity 1 — Habitat Hunt: Food, Water, Shelter, Space (20 min) — Req 1**
- *Four Components Mini-Lesson (7 min):* Introduce habitat = food + water + shelter + space. Show local examples (oak tree, pond edge, brush pile) and how removing one collapses the habitat. Teams name one local habitat and its four parts on poster.
- *Local Habitat Hunt Game + Microhabitat Study (13 min):* In teams of 3, Scouts hunt the meeting-space yard or hallway stations — under log, near downspout, sunny vs. shaded corner — with clipboard and magnifier, logging food, water, shelter, and space for insects, birds, or small mammals they find evidence of (tracks, webs, burrows, leaves eaten). Each Scout records at least one habitat with all four components. Report one finding to the den. Completes Req 1.

**Activity 2 — Pick an Animal & What Makes It Threatened/Endangered (15 min) — Req 2 & Req 3**
- *Pick an Animal (5 min):* Teams choose one threatened or endangered animal to carry through Reqs 3–5 (e.g., whooping crane, green sea turtle, local bat, monarch butterfly, or freshwater mussel). Record choice on worksheet — same animal for 3, 4, and 5.
- *Think Like a Conservationist (10 min):* Using fact sheets/tablets, each team identifies characteristics that classify an animal as threatened (likely to become endangered) vs. endangered (in danger of extinction) — small/low population, limited range, declining numbers, loss of habitat, and legal listing (Endangered Species Act). Teams highlight which apply to their animal (e.g., "only ~800 whooping cranes left, nests in one wetland"). Each Scout can name two characteristics — completes Req 2 and Req 3.

**Activity 3 — Why It Became Vulnerable & What's Being Done (15 min) — Req 4 & Req 5**
- *Endangered Animal Charades + Causes (7 min):* Teams act or sort cause cards — habitat loss, pollution, overharvest, invasive species, climate change — and match to their animal. Example: sea turtle → plastic/ocean trash + beach development; crane → wetland drainage. Discuss cause in Scout words. Completes Req 4.
- *Animal Protection Comic Strip (8 min):* Teams research what is currently being done to protect the animal — laws, refuges, breeding programs, nest protection, clean-water efforts — and draft a 3-panel comic strip showing the threat, the help, and the hope. Each team shares their strip. Completes Req 5.

**Closing (5 min)**
Gallery of comic strips + habitat posters, recap four habitat components and the chosen animal's story (characteristics, cause, protection). Preview Day 2 service project — what to wear, gloves, water, and how today's learning connects to hands-on help. Collect worksheets for den record.

## Day 2 — Conservation Service Project in Action (60 min) — Advanced / coordinated requirements HERE

*Outdoor at den site, park, or partner location. Partner host or site contact present. Gloves and safety glasses as needed. Adults 1:3.*

**Opening (5 min)**
Buddy check, attendance, SAFE + tool safety review, and partner welcome. Restate habitat components and how today's work directly supports the animal type researched (e.g., "bee hotels add shelter/space for pollinators whose habitat was lost").

**Activity 1 — Service Project Brief & Build/Clean/Plant (35 min) — Req 6**
Leader + site host brief the conservation purpose, scope, and safety (tool use, lifting, hydration, wildlife, and trash handling). Choose one age-appropriate project sized for 45–60 min with 7–9 Scouts:

- *Option A — Air Bee n' Bee (Bee Hotels):* Teams of 3 assemble pre-cut bee hotel kits — sand, stack bamboo/tubes, secure, and place at approved site per host direction (sheltered, east-facing, 3–5 ft high).
- *Option B — Build a Bat House:* Teams finish pre-cut bat house kits (adult handles any power drilling), sand, fasten, and stage for host to mount on pole/structure.
- *Option C — Clean Waterways:* Gloved teams with grabbers/buckets walk assigned stretch to collect aquatic trash, sort recyclables, and weigh/bag for disposal — no wading above knees, no wildlife handling.
- *Option D — Native Planting / Invasive Pull:* Teams plant native seedlings or pull marked invasives in a flagged area, mulch, and water.

Each Scout participates hands-on for the full block with continuous adult supervision. Host verifies. Completes Req 6 if finished here; if project is large (multiple houses, long waterway, or planting >30 min travel), complete a meaningful segment today and finish on auxiliary day — still counts as participation with a clear contribution.

**Activity 2 — Reflect & Record (15 min) — Req 6 wrap**
Circle reflection: Which habitat component did we help (food, water, shelter, space)? How does this help the threatened/endangered animal we studied? What would we do differently next time? Scouts add one sentence to den service log and sign the project card for the partner. Take group photo for den record (with permissions).

**Closing (5 min)**
Thank partner/host, return tools, pack out trash, and hydration break. Explain auxiliary overflow for Scouts who missed Day 2 or projects needing extra time — no additional research needed, just participation. Hand out completion notes and remind families of any follow-up care (watering schedule, monitoring).

## Auxiliary Day (if needed)

**Auxiliary Conservation Service Day (60–90 min) — OPTIONAL overflow / make-up.**

If the partner site is off-site, the project is large, weather interrupts Day 2, or Scouts missed the service block, schedule a 3rd auxiliary day at the partner location within two weeks. Same staffing and safety plan — partner host present, Service Project Planning Checklist on file, AHMRs, gloves/glasses/water/sunscreen/trash bags, and tool supervision per SAFE Project Tool Use. Run the same program: 10 min welcome + safety, 45–60 min service work, 15 min reflection and documentation. Scouts who missed Day 2 complete Req 6 here; Scouts who already participated may return as helpers but do not need to repeat for credit.

## Materials Summary

Habitat component cards, Local Habitat Hunt + Microhabitat sheets, magnifiers, clipboards, field guides/species fact sheets, tablets/laptops or library books, research worksheets + comic-strip templates, markers, attendance sheet, service project kits (bee hotel / bat house pre-cut lumber + bamboo + screws + sandpaper; or cleanup gloves + grabbers + bags + buckets; or native seedlings + trowels + mulch + watering cans), gloves (youth + adult), safety glasses, trash bags, water bottles, sunscreen, whistles, first-aid kit, AHMR/permission folder, den service log.

## Safety Notes

Use Scouting America SAFE Checklist and Service Project Planning Checklist at every meeting; review SAFE Project Tool Use for age-appropriate tools — provide training and continuous, qualified adult supervision, and follow the most restrictive manufacturer's guideline if conflicts arise. Review Guide to Safe Scouting and Age-Appropriate Guidelines; bring a first-aid kit, check AHMRs, and brief Scouts on tool safety (clamp, cut away, one user at a time), sun/heat (water, shade, sunscreen), and weather hazards. For off-site projects, secure 2-deep leadership, permissions, and partner site rules; enforce buddy system and no solo work. For cleanups, wear gloves, use grabbers, do not handle wildlife or hazardous waste, and wash hands after. Review Digital Safety for any online research. Be aware of local regulations that supersede Scouting America practices.
