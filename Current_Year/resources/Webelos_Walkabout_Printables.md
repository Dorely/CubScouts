## Trail First Aid Response Cards

One set per first-aid bag. Scouts use these while practicing; an adult always gets involved when someone is hurt on the trail.

<table class="card-grid">
<tr>
<td><strong>Blister</strong><br><br>1. Stop at the first hot spot and tell an adult.<br>2. Cover a hot spot with moleskin or tape.<br>3. Do not pop a blister. Keep it clean and cover it with a bandage or a moleskin donut.<br>4. Fix the cause: wet or wrinkled sock, loose laces.</td>
<td><strong>Sprained Ankle</strong><br><br>1. Stop. Have the hiker sit and keep weight off the ankle.<br>2. Get an adult.<br>3. <strong>R</strong>est, <strong>I</strong>ce or a cool wet cloth, <strong>C</strong>ompression with an elastic bandage (snug, not tight; toes stay pink and warm), <strong>E</strong>levation.<br>4. The adult decides how the hiker gets back.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Sunburn</strong><br><br>1. Move to shade and cover the skin.<br>2. Cool the skin with cool (not icy) water.<br>3. Apply aloe vera.<br>4. Drink water.<br>5. Prevent it: sunscreen, hat, and sun shirt.</td>
<td><strong>Dehydration</strong><br><br>Signs: thirst, headache, tiredness, dizziness, dark urine.<br>1. Rest in the shade.<br>2. Take small sips of water or a sports drink.<br>3. Tell an adult.<br>4. Prevent it: drink before you feel thirsty.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Heat Exhaustion</strong><br><br>Signs: heavy sweating, cool pale skin, headache, dizziness, upset stomach, weakness.<br>1. Move to shade; lie down with feet raised.<br>2. Loosen clothing; put cool, wet cloths on the skin.<br>3. Sip water if alert.<br>4. Get an adult.</td>
<td><strong>Heat Stroke — Emergency</strong><br><br>Signs: hot, red skin; confusion; acting strangely; fainting.<br>1. An adult calls 911 right away.<br>2. Move to shade.<br>3. Cool the person quickly with water and fanning.<br>4. Do not give drinks to someone who is confused or not awake.</td>
</tr>
</table>

## Six Essentials and Clothing Checklist

Send home after the planning meeting. Bring everything in a backpack to the Oak Glen hike.

**Cub Scout Six Essentials**

- [ ] First-aid kit
- [ ] Filled water bottle
- [ ] Flashlight
- [ ] Trail food
- [ ] Sun protection (sunscreen, hat, or sun shirt)
- [ ] Whistle

**Clothing for Oak Glen**

- [ ] Closed-toe shoes with good grip (sneakers or hiking shoes)
- [ ] Socks that fit without wrinkles
- [ ] Layers: Oak Glen is about 5,000 feet high and can be cooler than home in the morning
- [ ] Rain jacket if the forecast shows rain
- [ ] Field uniform or activity uniform, as the pack requests
