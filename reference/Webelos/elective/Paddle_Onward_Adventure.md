# Paddle Onward Adventure (Elective)

**Official Source**: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/paddle-onward/

## Snapshot of Adventure
Adult leaders supervising activities afloat must have completed Safety Afloat training within the previous two years. Cub Scout activities afloat are limited to council, district, pack, or den events on calm or gently flowing water that do not include float trips (expeditions). Safety Afloat standards apply to the use of canoes, kayaks, rowboats, rafts, floating tubes, sailboats, motorboats (including waterskiing), and other small craft (Stand Up Paddle Boards).

Getting out on the water and paddling around is a great way to enjoy the outdoors and go places that you can only get to with a paddle craft. The boundary waters of Minnesota, the shoals of the Florida Keys, and countless rivers and lakes across the United States are available to only those who choose to paddle there. In this Adventure, you can choose a canoe, kayak, or stand-up paddleboard to complete the requirements.

## Requirements

### Requirement 1
Before attempting requirements 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 for this Adventure, you must pass Scouting America swimmer test.

### Requirement 2
Pick a paddle craft for which to complete all requirements: canoe, kayak, or stand-up paddleboard.

### Requirement 3
Review Safety Afloat.

### Requirement 4
Demonstrate how to choose and properly wear a life jacket that is the correct size.

### Requirement 5
Jump feet first into water over your head while wearing a life jacket Then swim 25 feet wearing the life jacket.

### Requirement 6
Demonstrate how to enter and exit a canoe, kayak, or stand-up paddleboard safely.

### Requirement 7
Discuss what to do if your canoe or kayak tips over or you fall off your stand-up paddleboard.

### Requirement 8
Learn how to pick a paddle that is the right size for you. Explore how the paddle craft responds to moving the paddle.

### Requirement 9
Have 30 minutes, or more, of canoe, kayak, or stand-up paddleboard paddle time.

## Activity Ideas

### Requirement 1 Activities
- **Webelos Swimmers Test**: Cub Scouts swim for the Scouting America swimmer test.

### Requirement 2 Activities
- **Webelos Pick a Paddle**: Cub Scouts pick from one of the paddle crafts to complete remaining requirements.

### Requirement 3 Activities
- **Webelos Swimming Safely**: Cub Scouts review the attributes of being on the water safely.

### Requirement 4 Activities
- **Webelos Life Jacket Safety**: Cub Scouts learn about life jackets.

### Requirement 5 Activities
- **Webelos Float with Confidence**: Cub Scouts use life jacket in the water.

### Requirement 6 Activities
- **Webelos Launch and Depart Canoe**: Cub Scouts practice how to enter and exit a canoe safely.
- **Webelos Launch and Depart Kayak**: Cub Scouts practice how to enter and exit a kayak safely.
- **Webelos Launch and Depart Stand-up Paddle Board**: Cub Scouts practice how to launch and dismount a paddle board.

### Requirement 7 Activities
- **Webelos Canoe Resilience**: Cub Scouts learn what to do if their canoe tips over.
- **Webelos Kayak Resilience**: Cub Scouts learn what to do if their kayak tips over.
- **Webelos Stand-up Paddle Board Resilience**: Cub Scouts learn what to do if their paddle board tips over.

### Requirement 8 Activities
- **Webelos Canoe Paddle Perfect**: Cub Scouts pick a canoe paddle and learn how to use it.
- **Webelos Kayak Paddle Perfect**: Cub Scouts pick a kayak paddle and learn how to use it.
- **Webelos Stand-up Paddle Board Paddle Perfect**: Cub Scouts pick a stand-up paddle board paddle and learn how to use it.

### Requirement 9 Activities
- **Webelos Paddle**: Cub Scouts enjoy 30 minutes of paddle craft activity.

## Safety Notes
- Prior to any activity, use Scouting America SAFE Checklist to ensure the safety of all those involved.
- All participants in official Scouting America activities should become familiar with the Guide to Safe Scouting and applicable program literature or manuals.
- Be aware of state or local government regulations that supersede Scouting America practices, policies, and guidelines.
- Before a Scouting America group may engage in any watercraft activity adult leaders must complete Safety Afloat training, have a commitment card, and be dedicated to full compliance with all nine points of Safety Afloat. At least one of the adult leaders must be trained in CPR. Training may be obtained from my.scouting.org, at council summer camps, and at other council and district training events.
- Supervision by an adult, 21 or older, who is trained in Scouting America Safety Afloat. At least one leader must be trained in first aid which includes CPR. Any swimming done in conjunction with the activity must be within Scouting America Safe Swim Defense standards.
- Skill Proficiency — Canoeing, rowboating, and rafting for Cub Scouts is limited to council/district events on flat-water ponds or controlled lake areas free of powerboats and sailboats.
- Planning — Canoeing, rowboating, and rafting do not include trips or expeditions and are not to be conducted on running water.
- Life jackets — All persons engaged in activity on the open water must wear properly fitted U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jackets.
- Complete Scouting America Safety Afloat (30 minutes) training on my.scouting.org.
- Confirm enough adults who will serve as supervisors during the activity to maintain a ratio of one supervisor to every 5 Cub Scouts.
- Have buddy tags for everyone who will be near the water, youth and adults.
- During the Adventure: give an overview and instruction on the points of Safety Afloat prior to the activity; provide continuous, qualified adult supervision; all participants must wear a life jacket during boating activities. Review the Life Jackets / Personal Flotation Devices Safety Moment.
