# Webelos Walkabout Adventure

**Official Source**: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/webelos-walkabout/

## Snapshot of Adventure
Some places you can only get to if you walk. Walking is great exercise and a fun activity to do with your den or family. In the Webelos Walkabout Adventure, you'll learn how to prepare for a 2-mile walk, what you should bring along, and what you should do if there is an emergency. And when you are ready, take your walk!

## Requirements

### Requirement 1
Prepare for a 2-mile walk outside. Gather your Cub Scout Six Essentials and weather appropriate clothing and shoes.

### Requirement 2
Plan a 2-mile route for your walk.

### Requirement 3
Check the weather forecast for the time of your planned 2-mile walk.

### Requirement 4
Review the four points of Scouting America SAFE Checklist and how you will apply them on your 2-mile walk.

### Requirement 5
Demonstrate first aid for each of the following events that could occur on your 2-mile walk: blister, sprained ankle, sunburn, dehydration and heat related illness.

### Requirement 6
With your den, pack, or family, go on your 2-mile walk while practicing the Leave No Trace Principles for Kids and Outdoor Code.

### Requirement 7
After your 2-mile walk, discuss with your den what went well and what you would do differently next time.

## Activity Ideas

### Requirement 1 Activities
- **Cub Scout Essential Six Review**: Cub Scouts will review Cub Scout Six Essentials and appropriate clothing.

### Requirement 2 Activities
- **Digital Map**: Cub Scouts plan a route for a two-mile walk.
- **Plan A Route**: Cub Scouts will plan a route for the 2-mile walk.

### Requirement 3 Activities
- **What's The Forecast?**: Cub Scouts check the weather forecast for the 2-mile walk.

### Requirement 4 Activities
- **Safety First**: Cub Scouts review the four points of Scouting America SAFE checklist.

### Requirement 5 Activities
- **First Aid Demonstration**: Cub Scouts invite members from a Scouts Scouting America troop, Venturing crew, or Sea Scouts ship to a den meeting and allow them to demonstrate first aid.
- **First Responder Visit**: Cub Scouts visit a first responder to learn about first aid.
- **Trail First Aid**: Cub Scouts learn first aid for blisters, sprained ankles, sunburns, and dehydration.

### Requirement 6 Activities
- **The Two-Mile Walk**: Cub Scouts go on a two-mile walk.

### Requirement 7 Activities
- **Stop-Start-Continue**: Cub Scouts assess their two-mile walk.

## Official Activity Details

### Cub Scout Essential Six Review — Requirement 1
[Official activity](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/cub-scout-essential-six-review/) · Indoor
- **Supplies:** Each Scout brings a backpack, their own Cub Scout Six Essentials, and closed-toe shoes; Webelos handbook.
- **Directions:** Scouts find the Six Essentials list in the handbook, then pull out each item and name it, explain its purpose, and name alternatives (for example, sun protection can be sunscreen, a hat, or a sun shirt). Check that everyone wears closed-toe shoes, explain why they matter on a walk, and ask what other weather-appropriate clothing is needed.

### Digital Map and Plan A Route — Requirement 2
[Digital Map](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/digital-map/) · [Plan A Route](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/plan-a-route/) · Indoor
- **Supplies:** Digital Map uses a device with map software for every two or three Scouts. Plan A Route uses one printed trail map per Scout and highlighters. Both use the [Webelos Walkabout Plan](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/webelos/Webelos%20Walkabout%202%20Plan.pdf).
- **Before the meeting:** Identify an area for a two-mile walk and prepare maps with a visible map scale.
- **Directions:** Use the Scouts BSA "5 W's" of hike planning:
  1. **Where** are you going? Scouts find the map scale and highlight a route. Ask where the walk starts and ends, what can track distance during the walk, and how much time to allow.
  2. **When** will you return? Scouts help set start and end times.
  3. **Who** is hiking with you? List the partners and drivers.
  4. **Why** are you going? Name something interesting along the way instead of "because it is a requirement."
  5. **What** are you taking? Always carry the Cub Scout Six Essentials; decide what else is needed.
- Record the plan in the Webelos handbook or the Walkabout Plan and share it with Scouts and parents or legal guardians. The official page suggests AllTrails or Gaia GPS for trail maps.

### What's The Forecast? — Requirement 3
[Official activity](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/whats-the-forecast/) · Indoor
- **Supplies:** Smart device with a weather app that includes a forecast.
- **Directions:** Scouts look up the forecast for the date of the walk and answer: What kind of weather is expected? What will the temperature be? Is special clothing or gear needed? Why is it important to know the weather before a 2-mile walk?

### Safety First — Requirement 4
[Official activity](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/safety-first/) · Indoor
- **Supplies:** One printed [SAFE Checklist](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safe/) per Scout.
- **Directions:** Scouts take turns reading the four points, then discuss proper supervision, why risks are assessed, what the Annual Health and Medical Record contains and why leaders need it, why equipment must fit the activity, and how to apply the four points to the two-mile walk.

### First Aid Options — Requirement 5
- [Trail First Aid](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/trail-first-aid/) · Indoor
  - **Supplies:** Moleskin, adhesive bandages, elastic (Ace) bandage, aloe vera, sunscreen, water, sports drink, and a small bag to hold the first-aid items.
  - **Directions:** Write one injury on each slip of paper so every Scout has one. Scouts draw slips secretly, count off, and take a pretend walk around the meeting space. In order, each Scout acts out an injury (for example, "Ouch! I hurt my ankle; I think I sprained it."), and the den gives first aid with items from the bag before the walk resumes.
- [First Aid Demonstration](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/first-aid-demonstration/) · Indoor — Invite troop, crew, or ship members to demonstrate first aid for sunburn, sprained ankle, dehydration, and foot blisters. Scouts ask questions and send a thank-you note.
- [First Responder Visit](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/first-responder-visit/) · Travel — Schedule a firehouse or EMS visit. Bring Annual Health and Medical Records for all participants. Ask the first responders to explain first aid for sunburn, sprained ankle, dehydration, and foot blisters. Send a thank-you note.

### The Two-Mile Walk — Requirement 6
[Official activity](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/the-two-mile-walk/) · Outdoor
- **Supplies:** Cub Scout Six Essentials, closed-toe shoes, Annual Health and Medical Records for every youth and adult participant, a map of the walk, and the [Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace Principles for Kids reference sheet](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/webelos/Webelos%20Walkabout%206%20The%20Two%20Mile%20Walk.pdf) for each Scout.
- **Before the walk:** Remind Scouts to bring the Six Essentials and wear closed-toe shoes. Review health records for restrictions. Remind families of the date, time, and location one week before and again the day before.
- **Directions:** Before starting, Scouts silently read the Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace Principles for Kids and say how they will follow both on the walk. Then take the two-mile walk.

### Stop-Start-Continue — Requirement 7
[Official activity](https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/stop-start-continue/)
- **Supplies:** None.
- **Directions:** After the walk, ask what the den would **stop** doing (what made it harder), **start** doing (what could have gone better), and **continue** doing (what went right). Works best combined with the Requirement 6 activity.

## Safety Notes
- Prior to any activity, use Scouting America SAFE Checklist to ensure the safety of all those involved.
- All participants in official Scouting America activities should become familiar with the Guide to Safe Scouting and applicable program literature or manuals.
- Be aware of state or local government regulations that supersede Scouting America practices, policies, and guidelines.
- Before starting this Adventure:
  - Review Age Appropriate Guidelines for Scouting Activities.
  - Complete the Hazardous Weather training module (Position Specific Training for den leaders on my.scouting.org).
  - Watch the Weather Related Safety Moment video (1 minute 48 seconds).
  - Review Scouting America Annual Health and Medical Record for all Cub Scouts and adults going on the walk and check for any safety concerns.
  - If someone in the den carries an EpiPen due to severe allergies ensure at least one other adult knows how to administer it. Review the Safety Moment on anaphylaxis.
- During the Adventure:
  - Use the buddy system.
  - All adults are to provide active supervision.
- Before starting this Adventure, review Digital Safety and Online Scouting Activities.
