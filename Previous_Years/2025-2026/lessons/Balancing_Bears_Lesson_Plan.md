# Balancing Bears Adventure Lesson Plan

**Estimated Time to Complete: 1 den meeting (60 minutes)**

## ✅ Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Requirement 1:** Make trail mix using trail mix math
- [ ] **Requirement 2:** Identify an animal that has a pattern to its physical appearance. Investigate how the animal uses its pattern
- [ ] **Requirement 3:** Find examples of bilateral symmetry in nature
- [ ] **Requirement 4:** Find examples of radial symmetry in nature

## Denner Duties Today (Quick Guide)
- Lead opening: flag/Oath/Law with den leader
- Assist: warmup game at start; cooldown/reflection at end
- Materials: help set up, pass out, and collect supplies/snacks
- Transitions: serve as line leader and model Scout behavior
- Roster: help track denner rotation and mark attendance
- Closing: help with flag ceremony and announcements; support cleanup
- Games: Denner (fixed role) facilitates 1–2 games; round leaders rotate among Cubs

## Den Meeting Plan: Math, Patterns & Symmetry in Nature (60 minutes)

### Opening (5 minutes)
- Opening ceremony
- Quick talk: What does "balance" mean in math? (Equal on both sides)
- Introduce today's theme: finding balance and patterns in math and nature

### Activities

#### Activity 1: Trail Mix Math (15 minutes)
**Requirement 1: Make trail mix using trail mix math**
- Explain the activity: We'll use math to create our own trail mix
- Set up ingredient stations with measuring cups/scoops:
  - Raisins, peanuts (check allergies!), chocolate chips, pretzels, cereal, dried fruit
- Each Scout creates a "recipe equation":
  - Example: "2 scoops raisins + 3 scoops peanuts + 1 scoop chocolate chips = my trail mix"
  - Write their equation on a card
- Measure ingredients into individual bags following their equations
- Optional: Calculate total scoops, compare recipes, discuss fractions/ratios
- Enjoy eating the trail mix!

**Safety:** Check all food allergies before starting. Have allergy-safe alternatives ready.

#### Activity 2: Animal Patterns Investigation (12 minutes)
**Requirement 2: Identify an animal with a pattern and investigate how it uses it**
- Show pictures of animals with patterns:
  - Stripes: zebras, tigers, skunks, bees
  - Spots: leopards, dalmatians, ladybugs, deer (fawns)
  - Other patterns: giraffes, fish, snakes
- Each Scout (or small groups) picks one animal
- Discuss/research: How does the pattern help the animal?
  - Camouflage (blend in with surroundings)
  - Warning (bright colors say "don't eat me!")
  - Temperature regulation
  - Social recognition
- Scouts share one interesting fact about their animal's pattern
- Optional: Quick sketch of their chosen animal showing the pattern

#### Activity 3: Bilateral Symmetry Hunt (10 minutes)
**Requirement 3: Find examples of bilateral symmetry in nature**
- Explain bilateral symmetry: "If you draw a line down the middle, both sides match like a mirror"
- Show examples: butterfly, leaf, human face
- Indoor hunt: Find 3 things in the room with bilateral symmetry
  - Ideas: paper, scissors, chairs, books, faces
- If weather permits, go outside briefly to find natural examples:
  - Leaves, flowers, insects, rocks, feathers
- Scouts can take photos or collect leaves (Leave No Trace - only pick what's abundant)
- Share findings: What did you find? Where's the line of symmetry?

#### Activity 4: Radial Symmetry Hunt (10 minutes)
**Requirement 4: Find examples of radial symmetry in nature**
- Explain radial symmetry: "Lines of symmetry go out from the center like spokes on a wheel"
- Show examples: flower (daisy), starfish, snowflake, pinecone, orange slice
- Activity options (choose based on location/season):
  - **Indoor:** Look at pictures of radial symmetry, draw examples, examine cut fruits (orange, apple, starfruit)
  - **Outdoor:** Nature walk to find flowers, pinecones, seed pods with radial symmetry
- Count the symmetry lines: How many ways can you divide it and get matching halves?
- Scouts share: What radial symmetry did you find?

### Closing (8 minutes)
- Group discussion:
  - What was your favorite part? Trail mix math? Animal patterns? Symmetry hunt?
  - Where else might you see symmetry? (buildings, art, sports equipment)
  - Challenge: Find 3 more examples of symmetry at home this week
- Review what "balancing" means in math vs. nature
- Closing ceremony

## Denner-led Games (pick 1 if time allows; 5–8 minutes)

- **Pattern Memory**: Leader calls out a pattern (stomp, clap, stomp, clap). Scouts repeat. Add complexity (stomp, stomp, clap, jump). Who can remember the longest pattern?

- **Symmetry Freeze Dance**: Music plays, Scouts dance. When music stops, freeze in a symmetrical pose. Leader picks best symmetrical freeze.

## Materials Needed

### Requirement 1: Trail Mix Math
- Various trail mix ingredients (check allergies first):
  - Raisins, peanuts (or allergy-safe alternative), chocolate chips, mini pretzels, cereal (Cheerios, Chex), dried cranberries, sunflower seeds
- Measuring cups or scoops (1/4 cup works well)
- Small resealable bags or cups (one per Scout)
- Index cards and pencils (to write equations)
- Napkins, hand sanitizer

### Requirement 2: Animal Patterns
- Printed pictures or books showing animals with patterns
- Optional: tablets/computers for research
- Paper and colored pencils/crayons for sketching
- Reference books or printed fact sheets about pattern purposes

### Requirement 3 & 4: Symmetry Hunts
- Examples of bilateral and radial symmetry (pictures or objects)
- Cameras or tablets for photos (optional)
- Small collection bags for leaves/natural items (if outdoors)
- Clipboards and paper for sketching findings
- For indoor radial symmetry: fruits to cut (oranges, apples, starfruit), knife (adult use only)

### General
- First aid kit
- Water and cups
- Cleaning supplies for food activity

## Safety Notes
- **Food Safety:**
  - Review Food Allergies safety moment
  - Check all Scouting America Annual Health and Medical Records for food allergies
  - Ask about religious or cultural dietary concerns
  - Have EpiPen trained adult present if anyone has severe allergies
  - Review Keep Your Food Safe guide for proper food handling
  - Clean all surfaces and hands before food preparation
- Use Scouting America SAFE Checklist for all activities
- Review Digital Safety guidelines if using tablets/computers for research
- Before craft activities, review Craft Tips video (2:34)
- For outdoor activities:
  - Buddy system
  - Stay within boundaries
  - Adult supervision at all times
  - Leave No Trace principles (only collect abundant leaves, don't pick flowers)
- Adult cuts all fruit with sharp knife (Requirement 4)
