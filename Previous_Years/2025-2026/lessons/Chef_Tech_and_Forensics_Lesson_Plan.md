### Forensics Requirement 5: “What Color Is It?” (30 minutes)

**Science goal:** Scout how chromatography separates ink into component pigments, showing many colors can be in one color line.

#### Before the meeting (adult setup)
- Test each marker brand/color to make sure it separates well.
- Cut coffee filters into 1 inch wide strips.
- Pre-label each strip with scout number or name and a color space.
- Set up one water station and one dry table per cub.

#### Materials
- Coffee filters (1 per Cub Scout + extras for repeats)
- Ruler
- Scissors
- Non-permanent felt-tip markers (several brands and colors; black and brown work very well)
- Pencil
- 1 glass of water per Cub Scout (or a shared station)
- Paper plates for drying strips
- Observation sheet and pencils
- Timer or phone timer

#### Activity Steps
1. Explain chromatography in one minute: forensics teams use this to separate mixtures into parts, just like detectives separating evidence clues.
2. Give each Scout one pre-cut strip of paper filter.
3. Scout labels one end of the strip with marker color and brand in pencil.
4. On the label line (middle of strip), Scout draws a horizontal marker line.
5. Fold strip over water glass so the line stays above water.
6. Place strip so the folded tip is touching the water and wait.
7. Watch closely:
   - Water climbs through the paper.
   - Color pigments travel at different speeds and split into separate bands.
8. Remove strip when bands stop changing or water is near the top.
9. Dry strip on paper plate and repeat with 2–3 other markers.
10. Compare strips:
   - Which markers split into more colors?
   - Did two brands with same color look different?
   - Which colors moved farthest?
11. Record results in the notebook and share one “detective takeaway.”

#### Reflection Questions
- Which color looked like one color at first but was actually many colors?
- Which part traveled farthest and why might that happen?
- How could a scientist use this pattern in a real investigation?
- What extra evidence would you collect before making a final conclusion?

## Games (choose up to 8 Cubs; all are no-contact and safe indoors)

### 1. Detective Freeze Tag (Higher energy)
- **Setup:** 1-4 meters of playing space per scout, 2 boundaries.
- **How to play:** One Scout is Detective. When Detective says “evidence!” everyone runs and freezes. Last moving Scout is “confused witness.” Replace Detective with witness.
- **Duration:** 6–8 minutes.
- **Why kids like it:** fast, clear rules, easy transitions.

### 2. Recipe Relay (Moderate energy)
- **Setup:** Set up three stations: ingredient cards, spoon relay, and measuring station (using empty containers only).
- **How to play:** Teams move in rounds. At each station, a Scout performs one safe action (sort card, pass container, pretend measure with a dry cup) then tags next teammate.
- **Duration:** 8–10 minutes.
- **Why kids like it:** active but controlled, supports planning and teamwork.

### 3. Forensic Sit-Down: Ink Mystery (Low energy)
- **Setup:** Scouting circle with 3–4 “mystery” strips on a table.
- **How to play:** One Scout gives each clue card:
  - “This strip starts with one color but hides at least two bands.”
  - “This strip traveled farther up the paper.”
  - “This one has very similar bands.”
  Others guess the marker type.
- **Duration:** 6–8 minutes.
- **Why kids like it:** calm, observation focused, good for mixed energy.

### 4. Optional Fourth Choice: Color Commanders (very low energy, seated)
- **Setup:** Everyone seated with notebook and pencil.
- **How to play:** Adult says 2 instructions (hands, shoulders, feet). Scouts draw each pattern in order using tiny movement only.
- **Duration:** 4 minutes.
- **Why kids like it:** quiet cooldown and resets energy.
