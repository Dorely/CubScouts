# Balancing Bears and Bobcat Review Lesson Plan

**Estimated Time to Complete:** 60 minutes

---

## Requirements Covered

### Balancing Bears (Elective)
- [ ] Requirement 1: Make trail mix using trail mix math
- [ ] Requirement 2: Identify an animal with a pattern and investigate how it uses it
- [ ] Requirement 3: Find examples of bilateral symmetry in nature
- [ ] Requirement 4: Find examples of radial symmetry in nature

### Bobcat Badge Review (Den/Leader Requirements)
- [ ] Requirement 1: Get to know members of your den
- [ ] Requirement 2: Recite the Scout Oath and Law with your den and den leader
- [ ] Requirement 4: Create a den Code of Conduct
- [ ] Requirement 5: Learn about the denner position and responsibilities
- [ ] Requirement 6: Demonstrate the Cub Scout sign, salute, and handshake
- [ ] Requirement 7: Share with your den a time when you demonstrated "Do Your Best"

---

## Denner Duties

**Today's Denner:**
- Help distribute trail mix ingredients and materials
- Lead one of the symmetry hunts
- Lead a game during closing activities
- Assist with cleanup

---

## Meeting Plan

### Opening (5 minutes)
- **Flag Ceremony**
- **Recite Scout Oath and Law** together (Bobcat Req. 2 Review)
- **Review Cub Scout Sign, Salute, and Handshake** (Bobcat Req. 6 Review)
  - Have each Scout demonstrate all three
  - Provide gentle corrections as needed

---

### Bobcat Badge Review (15 minutes)

**Requirement 1 & 5 Review: Den Members and Denner Role (5 min)**
- Quick round-robin: Each Scout shares their name and one thing they've enjoyed doing in the den
- Review denner responsibilities: "What does our denner do each meeting?"
- Recognize today's denner and their duties

**Requirement 4 Review: Den Code of Conduct (5 min)**
- Review your den's Code of Conduct
- Ask: "Why did we create these rules? How do they help us?"
- Discuss how the den has been following the code

**Requirement 7 Review: Do Your Best Stories (5 min)**
- Each Scout shares a brief story (30 seconds) about a time they demonstrated "Do Your Best" since the last meeting
- This could be at school, home, or in Scouts
- Leader affirms each example

---

### Balancing Bears Activities (35 minutes)

**Requirement 1: Trail Mix Math (15 min)**

*Setup:*
- Cover tabletop and set out all ingredients (sealed) on the table
- Print and distribute Trail Mix Math worksheets and pencils

*Activity:*
1. **Introduce the concept:** Scouts will create trail mix using fractions. They can choose which ingredients to use from those available.
2. **Choose ingredients:** Scouts select which ingredients they want in their trail mix (they don't have to use all of them).
3. **Complete Question 1:** Count the total number of ingredients chosen. This is the **denominator** (bottom number of the fraction).
4. **Complete Question 2:** For each chosen ingredient, write "1" (representing 1 scoop) as the **numerator** (top number). Write "0" for ingredients not used. Ask a Scout to share their fraction (e.g., 1/8 if they chose 8 ingredients).
5. **Complete Question 3:** Practice adding fractions. Add the numerators (top numbers) together while keeping the denominator (bottom number) the same.
6. **Make first batch:** Scouts measure 1 scoop of each chosen ingredient and combine in their bowl.
7. **Add more scoops:** Explain that adding another scoop increases the total trail mix, so the denominator changes. Give Scouts the choice:
   - Add 1 scoop of a NEW ingredient (fraction: 1/new denominator)
   - Add a second scoop of an ingredient already used (numerator changes to 2)
8. **Check understanding:** "If we add all the numerators together, what should we get?" (Answer: the denominator)
9. **Finish trail mix:** Scouts add their chosen extra scoop and bag their trail mix.

**Requirement 2: Animal Patterns (10 min)**

*Setup:*
- Display pictures of animals with patterns: zebras, tigers, leopards, ladybugs, monarch butterflies, honeybees

*Activity:*
1. Show each animal picture
2. Scouts identify the pattern (stripes, spots, warning colors)
3. Discuss how the animal uses its pattern:
   - **Camouflage** (tigers blend into tall grass)
   - **Warning** (bright colors mean "don't eat me!")
   - **Recognition** (zebras recognize their family)
   - **Confusion** (stripes make it hard for predators to single out one zebra)
4. Each Scout picks their favorite patterned animal and shares why

**Requirement 3 & 4: Symmetry Hunt (10 min)**

*Setup:*
- Prepare examples of bilateral symmetry (leaves, butterfly pictures, human faces)
- Prepare examples of radial symmetry (flowers, starfish, cut oranges/apples)

*Activity:*
1. **Explain Bilateral Symmetry:** "Mirror symmetry - one line divides it into matching halves"
   - Demonstrate with a leaf
   - Scouts find bilateral symmetry examples around the meeting space (themselves, pictures, objects)

2. **Explain Radial Symmetry:** "Like the spokes of a wheel - a center point with parts radiating outward"
   - Cut an orange in half to show the segments
   - Scouts find radial symmetry examples (flowers in pictures, cut fruit, decorations)

3. **Denner-led Hunt:** Denner leads Scouts on a quick hunt around the room/outdoor space to find at least one example of each type of symmetry

4. Scouts share what they found

---

### Closing (5 minutes)
- **Reflection:** "What was your favorite part of today? What did you learn?"
- **Snack Time:** Enjoy the trail mix while discussing the meeting
- **Game (Denner-led):** Choose one:
  - **Symmetry Simon Says:** Only follow commands that create bilateral symmetry (e.g., "Put both hands on your head" but not "Put your left hand on your head")
  - **Pattern Tag:** Tagger must hop in a pattern (hop-hop-jump, repeat) while chasing
- **Closing Circle:** Scout Law or Oath
- **Reminders:** Share what Scouts should tell parents and any upcoming events
